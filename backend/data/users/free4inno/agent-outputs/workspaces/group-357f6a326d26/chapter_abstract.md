# Abstract

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has catalyzed a paradigm shift in autonomous agent systems, transforming them from rule-based architectures to sophisticated, learning-driven entities. At the core of this transformation lies the concept of "skills"—modular, executable capability units that enable agents to perform complex tasks through composition and specialization. Despite the growing body of research on agent skills, the field remains fragmented across representation formalisms, acquisition paradigms, and composition strategies. This paper provides a systematic survey of recent advances in agent skills across four critical dimensions: representation, acquisition, composition, and evaluation. We analyze symbolic, neural, and hybrid representation approaches, examine learning paradigms from demonstration, instruction, and reinforcement learning, and review composition strategies ranging from sequential planning to dynamic execution. Our analysis identifies five key research gaps: (1) lack of unified skill representation frameworks, (2) limited interpretability in neural skill representations, (3) insufficient mechanisms for cross-domain skill transfer, (4) absence of standardized evaluation benchmarks, and (5) challenges in scalable skill library management. We propose a unified framework for skill-based agents and outline future research directions including neuro-symbolic skill representations, meta-learning for skill acquisition, and benchmark development. This survey aims to provide researchers with a structured foundation for advancing the field of skill-based autonomous agents.

**Keywords**: Agent Skills, Skill Representation, Skill Learning, Skill Composition, LLM Agents, Autonomous Systems
